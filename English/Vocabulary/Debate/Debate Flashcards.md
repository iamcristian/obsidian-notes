### Assert
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb [Transitive, Regular]
- **Español:** Afirmar (con autoridad), aseverar
- **Definition:** To state a fact or belief confidently and forcefully.
- **Example:** *"The author asserts that moral values are objective rather than culturally relative."*
- **Collocations:** *assert one's rights, assert authority, assert that...*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Implica firmeza y seguridad en la afirmación, a menudo sin aportar pruebas inmediatas.
- **Word Family:** assertion (n), assertive (adj), assertively (adv)

---

### Contend
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb [Transitive/Intransitive]
- **Español:** Sostener (un argumento); lidiar con
- **Definition:** To assert a position strongly in an argument; to struggle with difficulty.
- **Example:** *"Critics contend that the proposed policy will stifle technological innovation."*
- **Collocations:** *contend that, contend with difficulties, a bone of contention*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Muy común en debates para contrastar tesis opuestas (*proponents contend... whereas critics argue...*).
- **Word Family:** contention (n), contentious (adj)

### Posit
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb [Transitive, Formal]
- **Español:** Postular, plantear como hipótesis
- **Definition:** To put forward as fact or as a basis for argument or principle.
- **Example:** *"Let us posit for the sake of argument that free will is entirely an illusion."*
- **Collocations:** *posit that, posit a theory, posit a link between*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Registro filosófico y académico C1/C2 para establecer premisas teóricas.
- **Word Family:** N/A

### Refute
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb [Transitive, Formal]
- **Español:** Refutar, demostrar que algo es falso
- **Definition:** To prove a statement, theory, or claim to be wrong or false using evidence.
- **Example:** *"The defense attorney presented forensic logs that completely refuted the prosecution's claims."*
- **Collocations:** *refute an argument, refute allegations, conclusively refute*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** En inglés formal riguroso, *refute* significa PROBAR con pruebas que algo es falso, no simplemente rechazarlo verbalmente (*deny*).
- **Word Family:** refutation (n), refutable (adj), irrefutable (adj)

### Concede
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb [Transitive/Intransitive]
- **Español:** Conceder (la razón), admitir a regañadientes
- **Definition:** To admit that something is true or valid after first denying or resisting it.
- **Example:** *"The politician conceded that the initial economic forecasts had been overly optimistic."*
- **Collocations:** *concede defeat, concede a point, concede that...*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Crucial en la concesión dialéctica (*Even if we concede that X is true, Y still holds...*).
- **Word Family:** concession (n)

---

### Rebut
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb [Transitive, Formal]
- **Español:** Rebatir, contraargumentar
- **Definition:** To claim or prove that evidence or an accusation is false.
- **Example:** *"She took the podium to rebut the charges leveled against her research methodology."*
- **Collocations:** *rebut an argument, rebut a claim, successfully rebut*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Similar a *refute*, pero *rebut* puede referirse al intento activo de responder sin que necesariamente se haya cerrado el debate.
- **Word Family:** rebuttal (n)

### Substantiate
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb [Transitive, Formal]
- **Español:** Fundamentar, corroborar con pruebas
- **Definition:** To provide evidence to support or prove the truth of a claim.
- **Example:** *"The whistleblower was unable to substantiate his allegations with primary documents."*
- **Collocations:** *substantiate a claim, substantiate a theory, fully substantiate*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Término clave de nivel C1 para debates de evidencia empírica.
- **Word Family:** substantiation (n), unsubstantiated (adj)

### Disprove
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb [Transitive]
- **Español:** Desmentir, demostrar la falsedad de
- **Definition:** To prove that something is false or incorrect through factual demonstration.
- **Example:** *"The clinical trial conclusively disproved the hypothesis that the drug caused side effects."*
- **Collocations:** *conclusively disprove, disprove a theory*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Más directo y empírico que *refute*.
- **Word Family:** disproof (n)

### Corroborate
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb [Transitive, Formal]
- **Español:** Corroborar, confirmar de forma independiente
- **Definition:** To confirm or give support to a statement, theory, or finding with additional evidence.
- **Example:** *"Recent satellite telemetry corroborates the findings of the environmental report."*
- **Collocations:** *corroborate evidence, corroborate a story, independently corroborate*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Implica que múltiples fuentes separadas coinciden en la misma verdad.
- **Word Family:** corroboration (n), corroborative (adj)

### Fallacy
?
- **Part of Speech:** Noun [Countable, Formal]
- **Español:** Falacia, razonamiento erróneo
- **Definition:** A mistaken belief, especially one based on unsound argument.
- **Example:** *"The assumption that correlation equals causation is a classic statistical fallacy."*
- **Collocations:** *logical fallacy, pathetic fallacy, commit a fallacy*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Sustantivo indispensable en ensayos críticos y debates retóricos.
- **Word Family:** fallacious (adj), fallaciously (adv)

### Premise
?

- **Español:** Premisa, postulado de partida
- **Definition:** A previous statement or proposition from which another is inferred or follows as a conclusion.
- **Example:** *"If the underlying premise of your economic model is flawed, all conclusions drawn from it are invalid."*
- **Collocations:** *basic premise, underlying premise, false premise*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** La base lógica sobre la que se construye cualquier deducción.
- **Word Family:** N/A

### Nuance

- **Español:** Matiz, sutileza

- **Example:** *"A skilled debater appreciates the subtle nuances of diplomatic language."*
- **Collocations:** *subtle nuance, capture every nuance, full of nuance*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Describe la habilidad de no ver los problemas en blanco y negro.

### Dichotomy
?
- **Part of Speech:** Noun [Countable, Formal]
- **Español:** Dicotomía, división en dos partes opuestas
- **Definition:** A division or contrast between two things that are or are represented as being opposed or entirely different.
- **Example:** *"The rigid dichotomy between theory and practice often breaks down in real-world software engineering."*

- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** *False dichotomy* es una de las falacias más comunes evaluadas en ensayos.
- **Word Family:** dichotomous (adj)

### Paradigm

- **Español:** Paradigma, modelo conceptual

- **Español:** Catalizador, agente de cambio

- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Metáfora científica aplicada a dinámicas sociales, políticas o tecnológicas.

### Specious
?
- **Part of Speech:** Adjective [Formal]
- **Español:** Engañoso, falaz (aparenta ser verdad pero es falso)
- **Definition:** Superficially plausible, but actually wrong; misleading in appearance.
- **Example:** *"The spokesperson's argument was specious, masking severe budget deficits behind complex jargon."*
- **Collocations:** *specious argument, specious reasoning, specious claim*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Adjetivo C2 de gran precisión para desmontar argumentos sofísticos.
- **Word Family:** speciously (adv), speciousness (n)

### Empirical
?
- **Part of Speech:** Adjective [Formal]
- **Español:** Empírico, basado en la observación y datos reales
- **Definition:** Based on, concerned with, or verifiable by observation or experience rather than theory or pure logic.
- **Example:** *"Theoretical arguments are insufficient; we need robust empirical data from load tests."*
- **Collocations:** *empirical evidence, empirical data, empirical research*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Se contrapone a *theoretical* o *speculative*.
- **Word Family:** empirically (adv), empiricism (n)

### Pragmatic
?
- **Part of Speech:** Adjective
- **Español:** Pragmático, orientado a la eficacia práctica
- **Definition:** Dealing with things sensibly and realistically in a way that is based on practical rather than theoretical considerations.
- **Example:** *"A pragmatic engineer chooses boring, reliable technologies over untested hype-driven frameworks."*
- **Collocations:** *pragmatic approach, pragmatic solution, pragmatic view*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Cualidad sumamente valorada en la ingeniería y la resolución de problemas reales.
- **Word Family:** pragmatically (adv), pragmatism (n)

### Dogmatic
?
- **Part of Speech:** Adjective [Derogatory]
- **Español:** Dogmático, inflexible en creencias
- **Definition:** Inclined to lay down principles as undeniably true, without consideration of evidence or the opinions of others.
- **Example:** *"A dogmatic adherence to rigid design patterns often leads to over-engineered, unreadable code."*
- **Collocations:** *dogmatic approach, dogmatic beliefs, dogmatically insist*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Connotación negativa de cerrazón mental frente a nueva evidencia.
- **Word Family:** dogma (n), dogmatism (n), dogmatically (adv)

- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Su antónimo con prefijo negativo es *unambiguous* (inequívoco).

### Irrefutable
?
- **Part of Speech:** Adjective [Formal]
- **Español:** Irrefutable, que no se puede negar

- **Example:** *"The database transaction audit logs provided irrefutable evidence of the insider attack."*
- **Collocations:** *irrefutable proof, irrefutable evidence, irrefutable logic*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Adjetivo derivado con prefijo *ir-* que denota solidez epistemológica absoluta.

### Antithetical
?
- **Part of Speech:** Adjective [Formal]
- **Español:** Antitético, directamente opuesto

- **Collocations:** *antithetical to, fundamentally antithetical*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Rige siempre la preposición *to* (*antithetical to*).

### Red herring
?
- **Part of Speech:** Noun [Countable, Idiomatic]
- **Español:** Pista falsa, maniobra de distracción
- **Definition:** Something, especially a clue, that is or is intended to be misleading or distracting from the real issue.
- **Example:** *"Focusing on minor variable naming conventions was a red herring that diverted attention from fatal architectural bugs."*
- **Collocations:** *introduce a red herring, be a red herring*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Falacia clásica de distracción en debates políticos y de ingeniería.

### Slippery slope
?
- **Part of Speech:** Noun [Countable, Idiomatic]
- **Español:** Pendiente resbaladiza (falacia de bola de nieve)
- **Definition:** An idea or course of action which will lead to something unacceptable, wrong, or disastrous through inevitable domino effect.
- **Example:** *"Claiming that allowing one exception to security policy will destroy all company safeguards is a classic slippery slope argument."*
- **Collocations:** *slippery slope argument, on a slippery slope*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Argumento que exagera las consecuencias en cadena sin justificar los pasos intermedios.
- **Word Family:** N/A

### Ad hominem
?
- **Part of Speech:** Adjective / Noun [Formal, Latin]
- **Español:** Ataque ad hominem (ataque personal contra el emisor)
- **Definition:** Directed against a person rather than the position they are maintaining.
- **Example:** *"Dismissing an engineer's performance critique based purely on their age is an ad hominem fallacy."*
- **Collocations:** *ad hominem attack, ad hominem argument*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Se utiliza para descalificar debates donde se insulta al oponente en lugar de discutir los datos.
- **Word Family:** N/A

---

### Straw man
?
- **Part of Speech:** Noun [Countable, Idiomatic]
- **Español:** Hombre de paja (caricaturizar la postura contraria)
- **Definition:** An intentionally misrepresented proposition that is set up because it is easier to defeat than an opponent's real argument.
- **Example:** *"You are attacking a straw man; I never argued that we should abolish unit tests, only that integration tests should take priority."*
- **Collocations:** *straw man argument, set up a straw man*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Una de las falacias más frecuentes en redes sociales y debates técnicos.
- **Word Family:** N/A

---

### Beg the question
?
- **Part of Speech:** Verb Phrase [Formal / Often Misused]
- **Español:** Petición de principio (asumir lo que se debe probar)
- **Definition:** To assume the truth of an argument or proposition without arguing for it; circular reasoning in premises.
- **Example:** *"To claim that the software is secure because our security team designed it begs the question."*
- **Collocations:** *begs the question of whether*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** En inglés culto, NO significa simplemente 'hace surgir la pregunta' (*raises the question*), sino asumir como cierta la conclusión dentro de la premisa.
- **Word Family:** N/A

### Equivocation
?
- **Part of Speech:** Noun [Uncountable, Formal]
- **Español:** Ambigüedad deliberada, jugar con el doble sentido
- **Definition:** The use of ambiguous language to conceal the truth or to avoid committing oneself; prevarication.
- **Example:** *"The executive's equivocation regarding server downtime eroded customer trust."*
- **Collocations:** *speak without equivocation, deliberate equivocation*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Cambiar el significado de una palabra a mitad de un razonamiento para engañar.
- **Word Family:** equivocate (v), equivocal (adj)

### False dilemma
?
- **Part of Speech:** Noun [Countable, Formal]
- **Español:** Falso dilema, falsa dicotomía
- **Definition:** A fallacy that presents only two options when other reasonable alternatives exist.
- **Example:** *"Presenting our options as either 'rebuilding the whole system from scratch' or 'living with daily crashes' is a false dilemma."*
- **Collocations:** *present a false dilemma, fall into a false dilemma*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Obligar a elegir entre blanco o negro cuando existen tonos grises.
- **Word Family:** N/A

### Circular reasoning
?
- **Part of Speech:** Noun [Uncountable]
- **Español:** Razonamiento circular
- **Definition:** A logical fallacy in which the reasoner begins with what they are trying to end with.
- **Example:** *"Arguing that the company code is optimal because smart developers wrote it, and they are smart because they wrote optimal code, is circular reasoning."*
- **Collocations:** *guilty of circular reasoning, fall into circular reasoning*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** La conclusión se apoya en la premisa y la premisa en la conclusión.
- **Word Family:** N/A
