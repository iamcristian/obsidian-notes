### Put up with
?
- **Part of Speech:** Three-Part Phrasal Verb [Inseparable, Transitive]
- **Español:** Tolerar, soportar, aguantar
- **Definition:** To tolerate or accept an unpleasant situation or person without complaining.
- **Example:** *"The engineering team could no longer put up with unstable staging servers."*
- **Collocations:** *put up with noise, put up with behavior, put up with delays*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Equivalente formal: *tolerate*. Nunca se pueden separar sus partículas.
- **Word Family:** N/A

### Look up to
?
- **Part of Speech:** Three-Part Phrasal Verb [Inseparable, Transitive]
- **Español:** Admirar, respetar profundamente
- **Definition:** To respect and admire someone, especially someone older or more experienced.
- **Example:** *"Junior developers naturally look up to principal architects who write clean code and mentor others."*
- **Collocations:** *look up to someone as a role model*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Su antónimo exacto es *look down on*.
- **Word Family:** N/A

### Look down on
?

- **Español:** Mirar por encima del hombro, despreciar
- **Definition:** To think that you are better or more important than someone else.
- **Example:** *"Senior engineers should never look down on newcomers struggling with terminal syntax."*
- **Collocations:** *look down on others, look down upon*
- **Nuance & Exam Tip:** Connotación arrogante o elitista.
- **Word Family:** N/A

### Bring up

### Break down

- **Español:** Averiarse (máquina); desglosar (datos/costes)
