### Confusable 001

> [!question] False Friend (ES <-> EN)
> The audit revealed that our _______ (actual / current) cloud spending is 40% over budget.
>
> **Pista:** actual / vigente en este momento
?
**Respuesta:** `current (vs actual = real / efectivo)`
- **Español:** current = actual (de ahora) | actual = real, verdadero, efectivo
- **Regla y Uso:** 'Current' refers to what is happening or in force right now; 'actual' emphasizes what is real or factual as opposed to estimated or supposed.
- **Trampa Común:** Spanish speakers see 'actual' and assume it means 'present-day', using it where English requires 'current' or 'present'.
- **Variantes / Contraste:** The actual cost of the renovation turned out to be double the initial quote.
- **Ejemplo Extra:** *"Under current regulations, all data transfers must be encrypted end to end."*

### Confusable 002

> [!question] False Friend (ES <-> EN)
> She is _______ (actually / currently) leading the cybersecurity task force while the director is on leave.
>
> **Pista:** actualmente / en este momento
?
**Respuesta:** `currently (vs actually = de hecho / en realidad)`
- **Español:** currently = actualmente (hoy en día) | actually = de hecho, en realidad
- **Regla y Uso:** 'Currently' locates an action in the present time; 'actually' introduces a surprising fact or polite correction.
- **Trampa Común:** Writing 'Actually, I live in Madrid' to mean 'At present, I live in Madrid' sounds like you are correcting someone's mistake.
- **Variantes / Contraste:** He looks intimidating in meetings, but he is actually quite approachable.
- **Ejemplo Extra:** *"The committee is currently reviewing three competing proposals for the merger."*

### Confusable 003

> [!question] False Friend (ES <-> EN)
> Delaying the product launch until the bugs are fixed seems like the most _______ (sensible / sensitive) decision.
>
> **Pista:** sensato / razonable / prudente
?
**Respuesta:** `sensible (vs sensitive = sensible / susceptible)`
- **Español:** sensible = sensato, prudente | sensitive = sensible, delicado, susceptible
- **Regla y Uso:** 'Sensible' describes practical good judgement; 'sensitive' describes emotional responsiveness, physical delicacy, or confidential data.
- **Trampa Común:** Because Spanish 'sensible' means easily moved or delicate, learners misuse English 'sensible' to describe emotional sensitivity.
- **Variantes / Contraste:** Please handle those client records carefully, as they contain highly sensitive personal data.
- **Ejemplo Extra:** *"Wearing waterproof boots on a muddy mountain trail was a sensible precaution."*

### Confusable 005

> [!question] False Friend (ES <-> EN)
> After months of deadlocked talks, both unions _______ (eventually / temporarily) agreed on a five-year pay deal.
>
> **Pista:** finalmente / al cabo del tiempo
?
**Respuesta:** `eventually (vs temporarily = temporalmente / eventualmente en ciertos contextos)`
- **Español:** eventually = finalmente, a la larga | temporarily = temporalmente, de forma provisional
- **Regla y Uso:** 'Eventually' always means 'in the end, after a long wait or series of events'; it never expresses uncertainty or short-term status.

- **Variantes / Contraste:** The main bridge is closed for repairs, so traffic is temporarily diverted through the tunnel.
- **Ejemplo Extra:** *"Persistent underinvestment will eventually erode the quality of public infrastructure."*

### Confusable 006

> [!question] False Friend (ES <-> EN)
> Over four hundred researchers are expected to _______ (assist / attend) next week's climate symposium.
>
> **Pista:** asistir a (un acto, clase o reunión)
?

- **Español:** attend = asistir a (ir a un evento) | assist = ayudar, auxiliar

### Confusable 009

>

?

### Confusable 031

> [!question] False Friend (ES <-> EN)
> Nearly eighty percent of candidates managed to _______ (approve / pass) the rigorous C1 writing paper on their first attempt.
>
> **Pista:** aprobar (un examen o asignatura)
?
